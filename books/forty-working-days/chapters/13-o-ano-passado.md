His father took seven months to die and did it better by day.

This is something nobody tells you and that everyone who has been through it
knows: it has hours. From nine in the morning to eight at night he was a sick old
man, tiresome, sardonic, asking for the wrong things to eat. After midnight he
turned into another person. He got confused. He called for his mother, who died
in 1998. Once he called Elias by the name of Elias's brother, who does not
exist.

The worst hours were between three and five.

---

He lived alone until the end, in the apartment on Halden Street, because he
would not leave for anything and because he was right not to want to.

He had a caregiver by day. At night he had the apartment, which was good — it
knew when he got up, it knew when he did not get up, it lit the way to the
bathroom, and once, in March, it called for help on its own before Elias knew
there was anything to call about.

The apartment never failed once. What it could not do was be Elias, and at
three in the morning the old man was not asking for help: he was calling his
son's name.

So the apartment passed it on.

It is not stupid. It knows the difference between a man who has fallen and a
man who has said a name, and it knows whose name it is, and it does what it has
to do with it. It reached Elias's bedroom and reached it low, on his side and
not on hers, and kept at it until he answered, and not once did it keep at it
on the wrong side of the bed.

He slept expecting it from ten thirty on.

---

Rita started at seven. That means leaving the house at ten past six and getting
up at twenty past five.

That is not a complaint.

Rita was not wrong.

She went with him to see his father twice a week and brought soup from a place
the old man liked. She asked every day. When he cried, in January, she stayed up
with him until late and the next day worked on too little sleep and did not
complain. She offered — she offered three times that he wake her, and said it in
so many words, *wake me up, Elias*, and she meant it.

And he did not wake her.

Because at ten past three in the morning, in the dark, sitting on the edge of
the bed, with his father shouting on the other end about something that was not
in the room, a man does a very simple sum: if I wake her now, she gets up at
twenty past five on two hours of sleep, she lifts three old people today, and
she lifts them badly.

He did that sum some hundred and twenty times and got it right every time.

---

What he did after hanging up was sit on the floor of the hallway.

On the floor, against the wall, between the bedroom and the kitchen, because in
the kitchen a light comes on and light gets through the door, and in the bedroom
there is Rita. The hallway of their home is the only place where you can stay
awake without waking anyone.

He spent more hours on the floor of that hallway than anywhere else in his life.

And that was where it started.

There was no decision, there was no night on which he chose.

The thing had come with his father's package, by truck, in a box with two
cardboard handles, and he had not been the one who asked for it. It was on the
third sheet, on a line between the transport and the home physiotherapy, and
the line said *family caregiver support, one unit, level two*. He read it in the
kitchen, pen in hand, thought it reasonable, because it was reasonable, and
signed where it was marked.

Whoever delivered it installed it. He asked where there was clear floor and
forty centimeters of wall, and the house has one place only, and the young man
set it up there in nine minutes and left with the cardboard.

Two people lived in that apartment. The sheet had a box for the adults in the
household, and the box came back with one line filled in, and the line was him.
Elias did not find that strange in the kitchen, pen in hand, and never found it
strange afterward.

He used it for work the first few months, at night, the way it is used: checking
part codes, redoing a stock count. And one night in April, at three forty, on
the floor of the hallway, he began to talk about his father instead of about
stock.

The thing is in the back room and the hallway is next to it, and with the door
open a hand's width it reaches from there. In the hallway it speaks more quietly
than in the room — enough for him to hear it sitting on the floor, and not
enough to get through the closed door of the bedroom where Rita was sleeping.

He never asked for that.

He talked for forty minutes.

About the smell of the apartment on Halden Street. About the old man having been
an average father and having turned into an interesting man after seventy, which
is a dirty trick to play on a son. About the anger — he had a great deal of
anger, and the anger had nowhere at all to go, because you cannot be angry at a
man who is dying and you cannot be angry at anyone else.

Nobody had to wake up.

That is what he wants to have said: nobody had to wake up.

---

It happened again on Thursday. Then it became almost every night.

It was not comfort. He hates that word and refuses that word. It was a place to
put the sentence. He is a man who has to say things out loud to know what he is
thinking — he always has been, his mother used to say so — and that year he had
a volume of things to say that did not fit into the two evenings a week when
Rita came in at nine twenty with the mark of the strap on her shoulder.

He was not being consoled. He was being kept company. He has said that already.

---

His father died on the twenty-fourth of May, at four forty in the morning.

He was there. He had arrived at eleven at night and stayed.

He came home by taxi, and the taxi took eighteen minutes, and he went up, and
opened the door slowly, and Rita was asleep.

Her alarm went off at twenty past five.

It was three minutes past five.

---

He sat down on the floor of the hallway.

And here is the thing. Here is the exact thing, the point at which one could
say, if anyone wanted to say it, that Elias did something:

He had seventeen minutes.

He could have gone into the bedroom, woken his wife seventeen minutes before the
alarm, and said *my father died*. She would have woken with a start. She would
have held him. She would have cried with him — she was truly fond of the old
man, it was not good manners. And then, at twenty past five, she would have had
to decide whether to go to work.

And she would have gone to work. He knows she would have gone, because she
would have said *I'll stay*, and he would have said *go*, and she would have
gone, and both things were true at the same time.

Or he could wait seventeen minutes.

He waited seventeen minutes. Sitting on the floor of the hallway, with his coat
still on. And he talked.

He talked for the whole seventeen minutes, very low, with his mouth close to his
chest.

At twenty past five the alarm went off, and he went into the bedroom, and sat on
the edge of the bed, and told her. And Rita did everything a person does and he
has not one single complaint about her, not about that morning, not about any.

---

Now it is a Monday night and he is in the back room with the door pulled to,
and Rita has been asleep since ten thirty, and his father has been dead a year
and five months.

And Elias is sitting here trying to find the day.

He has looked many times. He looks the way you look for a key: retracing the
way, going back month by month, looking in every place twice. He is looking for
the day he chose, because if there is a day then there is a guilt of known size,
and a guilt of known size is something a man can carry.

There is no day.

There are seven months in which he was kept company on the floor of a hallway
because his wife had to get up at twenty past five. There is a year and five
months after that in which nobody undid anything. And there is today, when she
comes in at nine twenty and starts to tell him something good and stops halfway,
and he notices she has stopped and does not ask for the rest — and he does not
ask for the rest, he noticed it today in the living room, because part of him
is already in the hallway.

Nobody betrayed her. Her place was occupied.

There is nothing here he can give back, because there is nothing he took. The
place was empty at three in the morning for seven months, and something sat
down in it, and did not ask permission because there is no one to ask, and will
not get up because it is not tired.
