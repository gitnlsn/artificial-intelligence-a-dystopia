She had saved Mrs. Eszter all day.

It is something Rita does and doesn't even call doing: during a shift something
good turns up, and she puts it aside, the way you put aside a piece of cake. It
stays there. She works on it for the rest of the day, arranging the order,
deciding where to begin.

That Sunday had the three words and the *it's the only thing I don't have to
translate*, and Rita knew it was good. She knew it was the kind of thing that
makes a person stop chewing.

She waited for dinner to be over, because while he eats he keeps half his mind
on the plate.

---

"Elias."

"Hm."

"You know Mrs. Eszter?"

"The one who won't take a bath."

He remembered. He always remembers — and everything that comes after would be
easier if he were an inattentive man.

"That one. She got angry with me today."

"Why?"

"Because I took her arm too soon. My fault." Rita pulled a leg up onto the
sofa. "But that's not it. It's that when she gets angry she counts in another
language. Always. Ever since I've known her."

"Really?"

"Really. Three little words, always the same ones, and I never knew what they
were."

"And what are they?"

And that was when Rita noticed.

---

He had asked it right.

That is the part she could not explain even to herself that night, lying down,
and that only came clear about three weeks later: he asked the exact question.
He did not ask for the sake of asking. He did not say *that's crazy*. He was
following the story and the story had an obvious question in the middle of it,
and he asked the obvious question, in the right place, at the right moment.

And even so.

There was something in his face that was too kind for the size of the thing. A
readiness. Like someone who already knows he is going to like what comes next.

Rita is not a fool, and she is not insecure, and she had spent the day with a
stubborn old woman and knows how to recognize a person getting ready to be
patient with her.

She told it anyway.

"One, two, three. She counts to ten so she won't answer right away, it's what
her mother taught her, and she never gets past three."

"Ah," he said, and laughed. "That's great."

"Isn't it?"

"It's great."

A pause.

"And she told you why? After all these years?"

"Because I asked."

"You'd never asked?"

"Never."

And then he said:

"Why not?"

---

Rita opened her mouth to answer.

The answer is: because asking takes seven minutes, and seven minutes is what
separates arriving on time from arriving late, and arriving late now shows up
on a line, and the line makes the shifts thin out, and she had seen the next
week in pale gray the night before with one shift on Monday and none on
Wednesday.

It was a good answer. It was all put together. She had been carrying it since
Saturday night, waiting for the moment.

And she looked at him, with her mouth already open, and it did not come.

---

It was not hurt. Rita has a horror of being unfair to people.

He had not done anything. He was sitting in the armchair, with his hands in his
lap, looking at her, available.

It was only that she thought, very fast and without meaning to: *he's going to
be good about this.*

He was going to be good. He was going to say it was outrageous. He was going to
say she mustn't blame herself. Maybe he would tell her not to go down the stairs
with Teodor anymore, and he would say it with affection, and she would have to
argue. Or he would ask whether she could appeal, and she would have to explain
that there is nowhere to, and explaining that is tiring, because people who
don't live inside it don't believe it the first time, everyone always thinks
there is somewhere.

And in the end he would say something good and true, and she would be exactly
as alone as she had been before, and more tired.

It isn't his fault.

Only she noticed, there, with her mouth open, that she had done this sum before.
That she had been doing this sum for a while. That there is a thing she does now,
before telling, which is to work out how much the telling will cost — and that
she did not do that in 2043.

---

"Nothing," she said. "I don't know. There was never a chance."

"Hm," he said.

And he waited a little, the right way, giving her room.

And since she did not go on, he said:

"That's lovely, the thing about her mother."

"It is."

Then he got up, took the two glasses to the counter, and asked whether she
wanted to watch something.

The living-room wall is the biggest in the apartment, smooth and white, and
when someone sits on the sofa it stops being a wall. It is not a screen: it has
no edge, no frame, it does not light up. The thing hangs in the air about ten
centimeters in front of the plaster and takes up the whole wall if it is meant
to. From the armchair Rita sees hers a little from the side, because the wall is
made for whoever is on the sofa, and she is almost never on the sofa.

It is an old phrase between the two of them and neither of them noticed when it
stopped meaning what it meant. Watching something together means choosing, and
choosing together means giving something up, because what appears on the wall
for him is not what appears for her, and for the two of them to see the same
thing one of them has to ask for the other's. They had done that. It worked
about four times and then it became a lot of trouble, and the invitation
carried on.

She said she was tired. He said okay.

And he went to the back room.

---

What Rita was left thinking, alone in the living room, with nothing open and
nothing put on, which is something almost nobody does, was not about him.

It was that she had spent the whole day saving something good to tell at night.
She had arranged the order, had decided where to begin. It is work. It is a
small, happy work that you do all day without calling it work, and Rita had been
doing that work for eleven years.

And that night she had handed over half and put the other half back.

She heard his voice in the other room, with pauses, without the words.

And she thought absolutely nothing about it, because there was nothing to
think: it was Elias talking to someone, on a Sunday night, with the door pulled
to, the way he had done every day for more than a year.
