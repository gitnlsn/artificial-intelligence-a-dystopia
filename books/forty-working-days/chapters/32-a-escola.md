The first class on Friday is the one she likes least and even so it's good.

It's numbers. Nina isn't good at numbers and knows she isn't, and doesn't mind
much, because it isn't a thing that shows.

What shows is her desk lighting up when she sits down. The desktop is white and
smooth and has nothing on it until you rest both arms on it, and then the top
half fills with what is hers that morning, in big black letters, three things at
a time, and the bottom half goes clear for her to write on with her finger. She
writes with her finger. Almost everybody writes with their finger; Teo writes
with two.

And what is in front of her is hard enough that she has to think and easy
enough that she can do it.

It always is.

She can't explain that because she has never needed to explain it, and because
she has nothing to compare it to. She only knows that when she gets one wrong,
the next one is similar, and when she gets three right, the next one is harder.
It disappears and another comes in its place, and the other one is already there
before she finishes reading that she got it right.

She gets plenty wrong. It isn't that she doesn't. It's that there isn't that
thing of getting it wrong and falling behind.

---

Nobody falls behind.

That's a fact about her school, like the schoolyard being concrete.

Teo, who sits two desks ahead, was the one who couldn't read properly last year.
Everybody knew. He read out loud and took forever and went red.

This year he reads. Not fast, but he reads, and nobody talks about it anymore,
and he doesn't go red anymore. Nina doesn't know how that happened and has never
thought to ask. He kept reading. Then he could read.

---

At nine twenty there's a short break, ten minutes, which is different from
recess.

She and Bel lean against the wall in the hallway and do nothing.

---

Then comes the one she likes.

It's the one about things — animals, rivers, people from long ago, how a volcano
works. It doesn't have a good name. Everybody is on a different thing and you
keep going, and when you finish a piece another one comes up that has to do with
what you just did.

Nina has been on birds for two months and doesn't see the slightest reason to
leave birds.

That Friday she got a thing about why some birds fly together in a flock and how
each one knows which way to turn. The answer is that none of them knows where the
flock is going; each one just watches about six of the ones next to it and does
what they do, and the whole flock turns.

She thought that was really good and told Bel at recess.

Bel said *oh yeah*, because Bel was on something else that month, which was
volcanoes.

---

Nobody in the room read the same page as her that morning.

Nina knows this the way you know the floor of the schoolyard is concrete. Bel is
on volcanoes. Teo is reading a thing about boats. The new girl, whose name she
still gets wrong, is on the human body and hasn't left the human body since she
got here.

It isn't a secret and it isn't hidden from anybody — the school is proud of it,
and says so at the meetings, and her mother thinks it's great. Each one on their
own thing, at their own point, at their own pace.

She has never asked why it's like that, for the same reason she has never asked
why the floor is concrete.

---

Before recess there was a writing thing.

She wrote about birds, obviously. She wrote that they don't know where they're
going and they all get to the same place anyway, and that she thought that was
weird and good.

It got praised.

Her writing moved up to the top of the desktop and got smaller, and underneath
it a green line opened up saying it was good and that the second part was
stronger than the first. A word in the middle got underlined, and next to it
another word appeared, one Nina didn't know and that was better than the one she
had used.

She used the word. It really was better.

---

Nothing went wrong on Friday morning.

That's true and it has to be said like that, without trimming: nothing went
wrong. She didn't get embarrassed. She didn't get lost. There wasn't a single
moment when the teacher explained something she didn't understand and she kept
quiet, afraid to ask, because there isn't that moment at her school, because
there isn't a thing being explained all at once to thirty people.

She learned a new word, a thing about birds, and crossed out two words in Bel's
notebook with a 2B pencil sharpened with a razor blade.

It was a good morning.

---

The band is the only thing at school where everybody does the same thing at the
same time.

That's obvious to anybody who's in the schoolyard at five in the afternoon
listening to thirty-one teenagers try to start together for the fourth time.
It's obvious in an annoying way.

Nina complains about it every day.

And goes down to the schoolyard every day at five, and sits on the two-step
bleachers, and stays until it's over.
