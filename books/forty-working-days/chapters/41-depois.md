It ended in a flat sort of way.

The last truck went by with the banner, and the crowd clapped, and then it
waited a few seconds, because nobody really knows when a parade is over, and
then one person left, and then everybody left at the same time.

---

The marshals took out a section of the barrier and the street turned into
people walking in every direction.

Nina climbed down from the gap with her legs asleep and nearly fell.

"Careful," said Grandpa.

"My leg went to sleep."

"So did mine."

"Yours went to sleep from being old."

"Yours went to sleep from standing on a barrier for an hour and a half."

---

Her mother came up from behind and said she had seen everything.

She had not seen everything. She was four meters away, leaning against the
wall, and Nina knows she did not see the band go by because Nina turned around
once to call her and her mother was behind three gray rectangles fanned open at
chin height, facing the other way. From where Nina was you could see the backs
of the three, which are smooth and have nothing on them, and her mother's chin
under them, low.

Nina did not say any of that.

"Did you see Bel?"

"I did, sweetheart. She looked lovely."

"She had her serious face on."

"She did."

---

They crossed to the pharmacy side because there was an ice cream cart that had
been parked there since eleven and Nina had brought up the ice cream four times.

She got lemon.

Grandpa did not want one and her mother took a bite of hers without asking,
which is a thing Nina hates and lets happen.

---

The street emptied out in front of them while they waited in line at the cart.

It is quick. In about ten minutes what had been a wall of people turned into a
lot of separate people going home — both ways, down the side streets, to the
bus stops — and each of them turned into an ordinary person crossing the
street.

A woman with a bag of bread. A man on his own in a coat. A young woman with a
big bag on her shoulder waiting for the light.

Nina did not look at any of them in particular.

---

The name of the pharmacy was back above the pharmacy.

The price of the ice cream was above the cart, and Nina checked it, the way you
check, and it was the same as always.

---

"Grandpa."

"Yes."

"Next week I'm telling what Bel said."

"About what?"

"About Lu. Lu said the robot was the size of a horse and it wasn't, and I'm
going to tell Bel, and Bel's going to be so happy."

"She'll be happy that Lu got it wrong?"

"She'll be **so** happy."

Grandpa laughed.

"You two are horrible."

"Lu makes things up," said Nina, with the complete authority of someone who is
right.

---

Her mother called the car and told them to wait at the corner.

They waited at the corner.

Grandpa stood with one hand on the lamppost and his hat in the other, and Nina
kept picking at the paper from the ice cream, which was stuck.

They did not talk.

It was not a silence about anything. It was only that the ice cream was melting
down one side and she was busy with that, and he was standing, and the sun had
come out for good, and the light at the corner turned and turned back about four
times with hardly anyone crossing.

---

The car came at twelve twenty.

Nina got in first and sat in the middle, which is her place when Grandpa comes
along, and Grandpa got in slowly and took a while to find the belt and her
mother waited.

"The sun got you," said her mother. "You two got some sun."

"I'm hungry," said Nina.

"You just had an ice cream."

"Ice cream isn't food."

Grandpa said he was hungry too.

---

They dropped him off in Marvik and went up with him because her mother wanted to
look at something in the bedroom window that wasn't working right.

Nina went into the kitchen, opened the cupboard above the sink, and the coconut
one was behind the milk one.

She took two and ate one on the way back to the car.

On Tuesday there would be rehearsal again, not the band's, not hers either, but
she would go down to the yard at five as always, and she would sit on the
two-step bleachers.
