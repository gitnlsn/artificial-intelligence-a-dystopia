Bel is unfair and Nina has known it since the second year of school.

It isn't a discovery. It's a feature, like Bel being tall. Bel decides things,
changes her mind, and when she changes her mind she acts like the idea was always
that one, and if you remember out loud that it wasn't she says *I never said
that*.

She never said that.

---

That Friday the fight was about the spot on the bleachers.

The schoolyard bleachers have two steps and about four good spots, which are the
ones in the shade of the tree, and Nina got there first and sat in one of them
and saved the one next to her with her backpack, which is the rule.

Bel came with Lu.

And Bel said, "Aw, Nina, go sit over there, come on."

Over there is the concrete.

---

Nina didn't say anything for about five seconds.

Then she said:

"I got here first."

"But Lu can't be in the sun because of her arm."

Lu's arm is an allergy she had in May that cleared up in May.

"Lu's arm cleared up."

"You're such a pain," said Bel.

---

Here is a thing about Bel: she knew she was wrong.

Nina saw it. You could see it on her face, in that half second before she said
*such a pain*, which is what Bel says when she knows she's wrong and isn't going
to back down in front of Lu.

And Nina, who is nine, understood that perfectly, and felt two things at the same
time — real anger and a strange sort of sorry for Bel — and stood up, and picked
up her backpack, and went and sat on the concrete.

---

She stayed on the concrete for twenty minutes.

It was horrible. She sat watching the two of them in the shade and imagining
things she was going to say and wasn't going to say, and each one of them was
better than the last.

She seriously thought about never talking to Bel again. She actually decided it.
She decided, with total clarity, that that had been the last time.

---

At twenty-two minutes Bel sat down on the concrete next to her.

She didn't say sorry. Bel doesn't say sorry; that doesn't exist and never has.

She sat down and said:

"Lu said her brother saw the robot that's going to be in the parade. The one
with four legs."

Nina didn't answer.

"She said it's the size of a horse."

"It's not the size of a horse."

"That's what she said."

"Lu makes stuff up."

"Lu makes stuff up **so much**," said Bel, with enormous pleasure.

And then it was over.

---

It was over just like that. With nothing. Without anybody saying who was wrong,
without a grown-up, without a talk.

The two of them stayed on the concrete — on the concrete, both of them, with the
four good spots empty on the other side, because Lu had gone off and neither of
them got up to move.

They stayed until the bell talking about Lu behind her back, which she didn't
deserve, and then talking about the band, and then about nothing.

---

Nina went back to class with concrete on her knee and didn't think about it
again.

This is what she would say if she told it: *it was just a fight*. She didn't hold
on to it. She didn't take it home. She didn't tell her mother in the car and
isn't going to tell Grandpa next Thursday, because by next Thursday something
else will have happened.

What was left, if anything was left, was so small that she has no name for it:

that Bel is unfair, and that she knows she is, and that she's Bel anyway.
