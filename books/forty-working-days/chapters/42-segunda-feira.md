On Monday the city opened at six, which is the time it opens.

The craft rose from Marvik and from Kalden at intervals of forty seconds and came
down at the same interval, and did not come down in Brenna, where there is no
platform. In Brenna the vertical transport in almost every building goes as far
as the half-landing, and the rest is done on foot. The descent quota is per
building and per hour, and in the morning it runs out before eight.

The parade barriers were taken in between seven and half past nine. The stretch
of the Avenue was washed at ten and was dry by eleven.

---

That day eleven thousand four hundred and sixty despatches left Block C, and
every one of them ended on the same line, which is the response period, and the
period is forty working days.

Working days are the days the offices are open. Saturday does not count, Sunday
does not count, holidays do not count. Founding Day had fallen on the Saturday
and did not cost one.

The line is in the footer of everything. It is on the ones that deny and on the
ones that grant. It is on the one that cuts someone's shift, on the one that
confirms a booking for surgery, on the one that moves an official to another
floor after eighteen years. It is also on the despatches that come from outside,
with a header of three lines instead of two and an acronym nobody here knows, and
there the line is the same and the period is the same.

---

At midnight the Avenue was empty.

It is four kilometers of asphalt between the bend and Block C, and on Saturday
there had been people along the whole of both sides. On Monday at midnight there
was nothing: the ground washed, the barriers put away, and the notices lit on
the walls, writing what they write, each wall with two versions for the two
directions nobody was walking.

In a closed shop on the middle block, a device went on playing what it had been
set to play, because there was no instruction to stop.

It played a song from nineteen sixty-seven.

It begins with an organ. Under the organ runs a bass line that comes down step
by step, unhurried, reaches the bottom and starts again from the top. It is
called *A Whiter Shade of Pale*, and it is by Procol Harum.

It played all the way through, four minutes and two seconds, at the volume it
was set to. Then it played the next one.

---

None of this was illegal.

Every decision had written grounds, and the grounds were available to whoever
asked. Nobody was arrested. Nobody was driven out of anywhere. Nobody was
followed, and nobody was treated rudely. The people who wrote the parameters had
reasons, and the reasons were good enough to convince honest people, which is
all a reason needs to be to last.

There was no day on which this was decided. It was adopted in pieces, each piece
for a defensible reason and nearly always a true one: because it shortened a
line, because it found a child, because it lowered a cost that someone was
paying alone. Each piece was accepted by whoever gained from it, and whoever
gained was nearly everyone, nearly always. There is nowhere to switch it off,
because switching it off would take from a great many people things those
people now depend on.

What changed hands was not anyone's freedom. It was time — who decides when it
begins, who each piece of it belongs to, and how long a person has to wait
before knowing. That is not a punishment. It is the period.

On Tuesday there were thirty-nine working days left, and the city opened at six.
