Boy, nineteen, Brenna.

He was asking for the removal of a preventive monitoring so that he could apply
for a place in a technical training program.

---

Preventive monitoring is not a penalty and is not on record as a prior. That is
written in the first line of the instrument itself, and Voss has read that line
some two hundred times.

It is a flag. It comes in by a composition of factors — address, age band,
family history of default, two incident records in the household in which the
boy does not appear as a party — and its practical effect is that certain
processes come to require additional analysis.

A training place with competition is one of those processes.

It does not bar. It delays. And since the ranking list closes on a fixed date,
the delay has, in practice, the effect of barring, which is written nowhere and
is known to everyone who works on the floor.

---

The appeal was nine lines long.

Nine lines is a lot. Most have two, or have none, because most people do not
know there is a field for the claim.

Voss read it.

The boy said that he had never been charged, that the two records were his
stepfather's and that he had not lived there since March, that he had full
attendance, that he was attaching the proof of full attendance, and that he had
nothing to present beyond that because nothing had happened.

*I have nothing to present because nothing happened.*

It was written like that, in those words, on the seventh line.

---

He stopped for two minutes and twenty seconds.

Voss times things without meaning to. It is something he has, a good sense of
duration, and he knows that two minutes and twenty seconds on a case like that
is far above normal.

He sat looking at the seventh line.

---

The difficulty is the following, and Voss can state it precisely, and that is
what makes it worse:

The instrument does not claim that the boy did anything. It claims that profiles
with that composition of factors show a higher incidence of a particular
outcome. That is true. Voss has seen the series; it is robust; he has no
technical objection to make.

And on the other side is a nineteen-year-old boy who needs to demonstrate that
he, individually, will not have that outcome.

There is no document that does that.

There are documents to prove what happened. There is no document to prove what
will not happen, and there cannot be, because the object is not in the world
yet. The proof of full attendance is good proof that he went to class, and going
to class is the past, and the instrument is not about his past.

All the boy can present is absence.

And the absence is already there. It is the reason he has not yet been charged,
and it is exactly what the instrument says is not enough.

---

He thought, briefly, of the list pinned to the wall in 2013 with eleven names.

It was a short thought and it passed.

This is important and should be recorded: Voss saw the likeness, saw the whole
of it, and felt nothing in particular. A boy from Brenna in front of one barrier
and himself, at seventeen, in front of another. He recognized the shape and went
on.

He can absorb injustice. He is trained. Injustice is something you see at this
desk forty times a day, and it does not stop anyone from working properly, and if
it did there would be no work and the queue would not move and the people who
are in the right would not be seen either.

---

He denied it.

Because the request was for removal of the flag, and the flag was correct — the
factors were there, checked, all verifiable, none of them invented. There was no
material error. There was no new fact. There was a boy saying that he is not his
profile, which is possibly true and is not subject to review.

---

Afterward he did something he had not done in years.

There is a field, at the end of the reviser's form, called *remarks*. It is not
mandatory. It does not go into the reply to the applicant. It is not read by the
applicant, nor by the second instance, nor by anyone Voss has met in twenty-two
years in the building.

He does not know where it goes. Nobody knows. There is a version, on the floor,
that the field was created in 2035 for some methodology review that never
happened.

Voss drew the field to the middle of the desk with two fingers. When there is
somewhere to write, the lower half of the desktop lights up in a slightly paler
gray, and what a person writes there rises into the field.

He wrote, unhurried:

*Applicant has no means of proof for the subject of the flag. The flag does not
describe any conduct of the applicant. It is not possible for him, by any
existing document, to demonstrate what he is required to demonstrate.*

He read it twice. He corrected a comma.

He touched two fingers to the corner of the field, and the field withdrew, and
the desktop went dark.

---

Then he denied fourteen more cases and went down to the square at twelve on the
dot.

In the evening he ate, put his plate in the slot, read twenty pages and turned off the
light at nine forty, and slept well, the way he sleeps.
