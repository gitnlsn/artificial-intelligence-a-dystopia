She laid out her clothes on the chair at eight at night.

Pants, the yellow T-shirt, and the thin jacket, because her mother said it was
going to be cold in the morning and Nina doesn't believe it but laid it out
anyway. And the sneakers under the chair, both pointing forward, the right way,
to push her feet in and go.

Then she stood looking at it.

---

"What time do we leave?"

"Eight thirty."

"Really eight thirty?"

"Eight thirty, Nina."

"Because we have to get Grandpa."

"I know."

"And he's slow."

"I know, sweetheart."

---

She went to bed at nine.

At nine twenty she went to get a drink of water.

At nine forty she went to the bathroom, and she didn't need to, and she went
anyway.

At ten her mother appeared at the door and said *sleep*, and Nina said *I am
sleeping*, and her mother said *you're talking to me*.

---

The problem was that she kept seeing it.

Not seeing like imagining something pretty. Seeing like organizing: the
barrier, the exact spot at the barrier, Grandpa on the right side because he
hears better on the right. Bel going by in the second row. Whether she could
yell Bel's name or whether it would mess things up. Whether Bel would be able to
look. Whether there'd be time to go to the bathroom first.

She decided she was going to yell Bel's name.

Then she undecided it, because Dória would see.

Then she decided it again.

---

At ten thirty she got up and checked that Grandpa's pencil was in the front
pocket of her backpack, and it was, and she wasn't going to take the backpack,
and she checked anyway.

At eleven o'clock she fell asleep.

---

She woke up at five twenty-three.

It was dark. It was completely dark, and there was no noise at all in the house,
and she lay there a few minutes with her eyes open working out how long was
left.

Three hours and seven minutes till they left.

She lay there a little longer.

Then she got up, went to the chair in the dark, and checked her clothes with her
hand — the pants, the T-shirt, the jacket, and the two sneakers pointing forward.

It was all there.

She went back to bed and didn't sleep anymore, and lay listening to the building
wake up: a pipe, a door, someone walking upstairs, and a little after six the
first bus on the street below.
