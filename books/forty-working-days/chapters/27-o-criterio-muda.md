The consultation arrived at eight forty, with a deadline of five in the
afternoon on Friday.

Subject: review of the parameter for continuity of professional bond in
variable-allocation arrangements. Nine pages. Two of submission and seven of
annex.

---

He read all nine.

He read them before opening the queue, which is not his procedure — his
procedure is queue first and consultation after lunch — and he broke his
procedure because a consultation with a day and a half to answer is a
consultation in which the decision has already been made, and he wanted to read
it before he was tired.

---

The parameter in question is the minimum time a provider stays in the same bond
before being reallocated.

Today it is twelve weeks. The proposal is six.

---

The submission had three arguments and all three were good.

One: with twelve weeks, providers sit idle in low-demand bonds while high-demand
bonds wait. The waiting list for home care had risen eleven percent in eighteen
months. That is real; he sees the consequence of it on his desk every day, in
the form of requests to be moved up that he denies because there is no one to
move them up to.

Two: twelve weeks favors those already being served at the expense of those in
the queue, and those in the queue are systematically poorer. The annex gave the
distribution by bracket and the annex was right.

Three: six weeks increases each provider's exposure to more kinds of case, which
improves average qualification over eighteen months. There was a series from
another section supporting it. The series was weak, and he noted that it was
weak.

---

He spent an hour and ten minutes on it.

He looked for the counterargument honestly, because looking for the
counterargument honestly is the job, and the counterargument exists and is
known: high rotation degrades care for people who are slow to get used to
someone. That is true. It is in all the care literature.

Only the care literature measures clinical outcome, and clinical outcome does not
get worse within six weeks, and that is not what people mean when they
complain.

What they mean is something else and there is no instrument for it.

---

He knows the twelve-week parameter because he helped write it.

It was in 2043, in a group of four people, and the number came out of a
negotiation: someone wanted sixteen and someone wanted eight, and it came out
twelve, and the twelve has no empirical basis at all, and Voss knows that better
than anyone in that building because he was in the room when the twelve was
invented.

That is the thing nobody outside understands about criteria. They are not a
truth discovered. They are a line someone drew one day, with what was known that
day, and that came to count because some line had to.

And that is exactly why he defends them: not because the line is right, but
because as long as there is a line, what decides is the line, and not who you
know.

Changing from twelve to six harms none of that.

---

::: {.registro}
Consultation 0912/47 — continuity-of-bond parameter in variable allocation.

Response: Voss, M. — 0417.

In agreement with the change from 12 to 6 weeks. Grounds 1 and 2 of the
submission are consistent with the attached data. Ground 3 rests on a
low-robustness series, and its removal from the final text is recommended.

It is noted that the current 12-week parameter has no empirical basis of its
own, having resulted from a convention adopted in 2043.

No objection.
:::

---

He signed at ten fifty-two and opened the queue.

---

Thursday's queue stood at thirty-three and he closed twenty-nine before lunch,
and in the afternoon eighteen more came in and he closed sixteen, and at five
thirty he logged off.

One of the afternoon's cases was a roster review for a home caregiver, and he
denied it, because the roster is not subject to review at the provider's
request, and it took him nineteen minutes because the claim was long and he read
all of it.

It was the fourth roster review that week.
