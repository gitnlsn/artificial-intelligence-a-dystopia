He sat down at nine forty with the form open and stayed until ten past four.

It was not anguish. That has to be said at the outset, because the word anguish
would describe the night badly: he worked. He sat the way he sits to work, with
the living-room light, with the notebook beside him, and he spent six and a
half hours doing the thing he knows how to do better than anyone in that
building, which is to put together a response.

And he put together a good one.

---

He knows the three doors.

Every response goes through one of the three, and people who do not know this
write nine lines of feeling and are denied in four minutes, and Voss spent
eighteen years denying those nine lines.

The first is material error: the act misapplied the rule. It will not do. He
checked the classification on Friday morning, with his coat over his arm, and
the classification is right.

The second is procedural defect: the reasons were missing, the authority was
missing, the deadline was missing. It will not do. The communication gives its
reasons in three lines, came from the board, and came in at one minute past
midnight.

The third is new fact.

---

The new fact was possible.

He found it at twenty past eleven at night and it was a good find, of the kind
he would have recognized and praised if it had come from someone else: the
redistribution cites opinion 0912/47 as its grounds, and 0912/47 deals with
continuity of bond between **provider and beneficiary** — a care relationship,
between two people.

The posting of an official to a matter is not a care relationship. It is
something else. The analogy is reasonable and it is an analogy, and an analogy
is not the object of the opinion, and a well-written response could argue that
the opinion was applied by extension to a case it does not describe.

He wrote that. It took an hour and forty minutes. It came to four paragraphs,
with the exact citation, with the object of the opinion delimited, and without
a single word about himself.

At five past one in the morning he had in his hands a response that, on his
desk, he would have read with attention.

He might have granted it. Probably not. But he would have read it with
attention, and written something in the despatch, and that, in his line of
work, is a great deal.

---

He read it through once, under his breath, the way he reads.

Then he sat still.

---

The problem was not in the text. The text was right.

The problem is that Voss knows exactly what it is.

It is a man who spent eighteen years saying that prolonged specialization is
not among the posting criteria, and that qualification is single and is not
subdivided, and that preference is not a subject for review — and who, the
first time the rule pointed at him, found in an hour and forty minutes a
technical distinction good enough to get him out from under it.

He did not find that distinction because it is true.

He found it because he looked. And he looked because it was him.

And that is the thing he cannot get around, by any route, and he tried for
three hours: none of the applicants that week had a Friday night to look. The
nineteen-year-old wrote nine lines saying he had nothing to present because
nothing had happened, and he was right, and writing better was not available
to him.

The capacity to formulate the reason. He had said that himself, in a meeting,
in 2044, and regretted saying it out loud because it sounded harsh.

It is distributed exactly like everything else.

---

At two thirty he went to the kitchen and stood in the dark, which is something
he did twice that week and never before in his life.

The alternative he examined, and examined seriously, was this: what if the
distinction *is* true?

Because it might be. An opinion about care applied by extension to the posting
of an official — that is fragile, and if some applicant raised that argument he
would raise an eyebrow and read it again.

And there Voss arrived at the place where the night ended.

If the distinction is true, it is true for every official redistributed on
those grounds. There are, he worked out in his head, somewhere between forty
and sixty people in that building in the last two years. None of them knows the
distinction exists.

He knows because he has eighteen years of exceptions review and a free night.

To file a response, alone, on an argument that only he is in a position to
formulate, and get out from under a rule that goes on falling on forty people
who have no way to formulate it — that has a name, and the name is exactly the
name of the thing he has been against his whole life.

It is not being treated with justice.

It is being treated as an exception.

---

He went back to the table at five past three.

He sat looking at his text, the four paragraphs, the exact citation.

And then he thought of her.

---

It made no sense to think of her at that moment and he knew it made no sense,
and he thought of her anyway, and it is the one thing from that night he could
not fit under any heading.

A woman in a gray coat on a wet sidewalk, with a paper folded in three in her
left hand, handing a secondhand book back to a man who had denied something of
hers that same day. Four seconds that were hers. And she said don't mention it.

She had nothing to gain.

And Voss, at five past three in the morning, with a finished response on the
living-room table, understood
that he did.

---

He did not delete the text. That he did not do, and perhaps he should have.

He saved it to his own history, with the date in the name, which is where you
keep a thing so that someone else can find it later. It took him a minute
longer than it needed to and he checked the file name twice.

Then he closed it.

Then he opened the response form, went to the field, and left it blank.

He did not write *noted*. He did not write anything. The field is optional and
he left it optional.

He put his thumb on the box that says he has taken cognizance, and held the
second it asks for, and the box closed.

He sent it at nineteen minutes past three in the morning.

---

One thing, he did.

Before closing, he opened communication 4415-P again and went down to the end,
to the reviser's remarks field — the field that is not mandatory, that does not
go into the reply to anyone, that he had used once that week and before that
had not used in years, and that nobody he has known in twenty-two years has
ever read.

He wrote:

*The distinction between a care relationship and the posting of an official
was not examined. It is probably relevant. I did not raise it because it would
not be available to an applicant without technical training in the matter, and
the act reaches other officials in an identical situation.*

He read it twice. He corrected a comma.

He touched two fingers to the corner of the field, and the field drew in, and
the room was dark again.

---

At ten past four he turned out the light.

He did not sleep, and it was the first night of his life that this had happened
for a reason he could not name, but he lay quiet until six, which is a thing
one does.

He got up at six. He made coffee.

The parade was at twelve minutes past ten and went by four blocks from his
home, and he had not the slightest reason to go, and he decided nothing about
it that morning.
