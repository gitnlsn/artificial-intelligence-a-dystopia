The school bus takes the long route on Friday because the avenue is closed for
the barriers.

Nina likes the long route. It goes over the little bridge, it goes past the blue
building, and it goes past that lot where there is a dog that always stands on
top of a pile of rubble as if it owned the place.

The dog was there.

---

She and Bel sit at the back, on the right side, because the market goes by on
the right side.

In front of the market there is a big, flat wall and that is where the things go
by.

The wall is cinder block painted beige and it is about twelve meters long, with
no windows at all, and it is the biggest flat wall on the whole way. The things
do not stick to it. They sit a little in front of it, a hand's width maybe, and
so when the bus moves they turn with it and you can see that they have sides.
Once Nina saw one from underneath, walking with her mother, and underneath there
is nothing: the thing stops, and the beige wall goes on.

---

That Friday the girl with the hair went by.

Nina saw her from far off, about thirty meters before, and was already getting
excited because it had been about two weeks since she went by. She is a girl of
about fifteen, with short hair, and she does a thing where her hair turns into
birds. It is not fast. She turns her head slowly and her hair lets the birds go
one by one and the birds disappear at the edge of the wall.

It lasts about eight seconds.

Nina saw the whole thing, her forehead almost touching the glass.

"Bel! The hair one went by!"

"Huh?"

"The hair one. With the birds. Did you see?"

Bel looked at the wall, which was already behind them.

"The car one went by," she said.

"What car?"

"The blue car that opens. The one where the door opens up."

"No car went by."

"Yes it did, Nina. It just went by."

---

The two of them stayed like that for a second.

And then Bel said:

"You saw a bird because you're always on that bird thing."

And Nina said:

"Oh yeah? And you're always on cars."

And Bel said:

"I'm not always on cars."

And Nina said:

"Yes you are."

And the two of them laughed, and Bel gave her a shove on the shoulder, and that
was the end of it, because the bus was coming up to the little bridge and from
the little bridge you can see the river.

---

There was nothing strange about it.

Nobody on that bus found it strange: two nine-year-old girls looked at the same wall in the same second, saw
different things, argued for thirty seconds, and decided that the difference was
because one of them likes birds and the other one likes cars.

Which is, as it happens, more or less true.

---

Nina got off at her stop and Bel went on three more.

Before she got off she said:

"Saturday are you going to be with the band the whole morning?"

"The whole morning."

"I'm going to be at the front."

"You won't get to be very far to the front. There's barriers."

"I'm going to be at the barrier."

"Okay."

"Bel."

"Yeah."

"If it goes wrong I won't laugh."

"You'll laugh."

"I will," Nina admitted.

And got off.
