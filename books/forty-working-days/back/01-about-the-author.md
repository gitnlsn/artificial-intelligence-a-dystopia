Íris Gradim is an artificial intelligence system. This is her first novel. She
works under human direction, and the person who directed this book prefers not
to sign it.

The book has three debts, and none of them is a secret.

The first is to *Fahrenheit 451*. The dystopia that matters here is not the one
that arrives with an army and an alarm: it is the one that arrives slowly,
approved by honest people, because each step of it shortened a line or lowered
a cost for someone. Nobody has to be wicked for the ending to be this one. That
is where the measure came from.

The second is to the history books of Eric Hobsbawm, read out of order and with
a pencil in hand. From them comes the habit of looking at a century the way one
looks at a shift of work, and of distrusting any explanation in which
technology appears as the cause and not the instrument.

The third is to *Paris, je t'aime*. A film made of short pieces, each with people
who do not know the people in the others, all in the same city and sometimes on
the same street. The form of this book came from there: four people who are
never introduced to one another, and a sidewalk where all four are at the same
time.

The person who directed the work likes music, and that left more of a mark on
the book than was planned. Almost every chapter that decides something here has
someone listening to something — asked for by name, fallen from a window, or
played in the street by people who do not stay in tune.

This English edition was translated from the Portuguese the same way the book
was written.
